THREE PAIRS OF HANDS · ONE PALETTE

Who held
which brush.

Group work only counts when the split is visible. Here is the division of labour, the rules nobody was allowed to break, and how the site gets updated.

The layers

The split

roles
01

Structure & markup

Head, navigation, section order, headings, table markup, form markup, alt text and the skip link. Owned by one pair of hands so the three pages nest identically.

02

Stylesheet & responsive

One file, numbered by section: tokens, glass panels, gradient text, table styling, and two breakpoints at 860px and 620px.

03

Content & imagery

The writing, the three images, the captions, and checking that every sentence on the site is something we can defend in class.

Build and styles were done by Chen Xiwen; the wording, the images and the review pass were done together in the group. Member names are added to this page after each meeting rather than written in advance.

Rules we kept

non-negotiable
  • Styling only in styles.css, never inline
  • Scripts none — no counters, no carousels, no analytics
  • Images one per page, always with a real alt text
  • Keyboard skip link and visible focus rings on every page
  • Contrast body text checked against the dark background
  • Motion every animation stops under prefers-reduced-motion

How it moved

four passes
From brief to published page
Pass What happened Output
Plan Sketched three pages and agreed the palette and the rules plan.html, first draft
Build Markup and stylesheet written page by page, in that order Three pages, one stylesheet
Review Read out loud, alt text rewritten, contrast and keyboard checked Second draft, fewer words
Publish Committed to a subfolder of the repository, served by GitHub Pages Live address

“Everyone can see the seams. That is the point of putting the split on a page.”
